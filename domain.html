01 / Literature

Literature survey

What existing research and products already cover.

Existing aquaculture work has investigated connected sensing, machine vision, fish-health screening and data-driven monitoring. For Discus management, the challenge is not simply to build a new classifier: it is to bring environmental, health, reproductive and physical loading information into a coherent workflow while preserving the limitations of each data type.

Water sensing & risk

Water monitoring can combine instrument readings, manual test values and visual appearance. Water-image categories are not substitutes for measured chemical concentrations.

Visual disease analysis

Image classification and object detection support screening of selected visible conditions. Species verification, symptom context and source-associated explanations help prevent overinterpretation.

Discus breeding biology

Breeding readiness, egg development and fry survival depend on behaviour, environmental conditions and observations across time. Snapshot images alone provide incomplete context.

Fish growth & tank loading

Fish-count-only assessments overlook size differences. Image-based calibrated measurement, biomass estimation and forecasting provide additional information about crowding.

Full reference list: see the proposal document in Documents.

02 / Opportunity

Research gap

What is missing from research and from products.

Core gap

A shared, evidence-aware Discus management workflow is needed.

The central research gap is the fragmentation of heterogeneous aquarium information: water measurements and appearance, fish-health imagery, breeding observations and fish-size-aware loading. An integrated workflow needs shared records and clear attribution without merging the outputs into one unsupported prediction.

01

Four distinct analytical concerns

Water quality, disease screening, breeding lifecycle and measurement/crowding each have different inputs and decision targets.

02

Methodological integration gap

Images, measured readings, timelines and calibrated dimensions require different validation, units and uncertainty communication.

03

Shared workflow gap

Users need a coherent mobile experience and traceable outputs instead of disconnected tools and unsupported, source-free recommendations.

03 / Challenge

Research problem

The central challenge for Discus keepers and breeders.

Problem statement: Discus keepers often judge water conditions, visible health, breeding stage and tank stocking separately. This fragmentation makes it difficult to relate observations to the right tank, time and responsible analytical method.

Research question

How can specialised sensing, visual analysis, predictive models and source-aware explanation be combined into a coherent Discus management workflow without treating distinct outputs as interchangeable?

Scope and limits

  • Users include Discus owners, breeders and ornamental-fish shop operators.
  • Outputs are advisory decision support, not veterinary diagnosis or automatic hardware control.
  • Image quality, calibration, available water measurements and relevant tank history affect results; the project does not claim exact spawning-date prediction.
04 / Direction

Research objectives

Main objective: one unified, explainable decision-support framework delivered through one backend and one mobile app.

01

Water quality

Monitor water quality over time and predict risk from stored sensor history and images.

02

Disease

Detect Discus diseases visually, explain the result and give retrieval-grounded treatment guidance.

03

Breeding lifecycle

Follow each tank through pre-spawning, egg and fry stages using longitudinal AI, safety rules, XAI and RAG.

04

Measurement and crowding

Estimate fish size and biomass from images and assess current and future crowding risk.

05

Evaluation

Evaluate each component with metrics suited to its task, and report limitations openly.

05 / Research process

Methodology

The four components share a mobile-oriented interface and FastAPI service framework while using separate, appropriate models and validation procedures. Water data, fish images, breeder observations and calibrated measurements are routed to their responsible analytical branch; explanations and limits remain identifiable.

MEMBER 01

Water quality monitoring

EfficientNet-B0 water appearance classification, Gradient Boosting structured water-risk modelling, and available sensor or manual readings.

MEMBER 02

Disease analysis

Discus species verification, YOLOv8s screening, Grad-CAM, symptom comparison and retrieval-grounded guidance.

MEMBER 03

Breeding lifecycle

XGBoost/SHAP readiness, EfficientNet egg assessment, fry anomaly and forecast concepts, and tank-cycle context.

MEMBER 04

Measurement & crowding

YOLOv8n calibrated measurement, derived biomass, XGBoost current risk and Random Forest future-biomass forecasts.

Component-level research

Four research streams, one project.

Each component has its own data, methods and evaluation. Expand any research profile to read its methodology and documented findings; all four contributions are presented at the same level.

Make Discus-specific visual health screening more interpretable by linking image signals with species checks, symptoms and knowledge sources.

Species-verification test accuracy: 98.17%. YOLOv8s validation mAP@50: 68.37%. These measure separate tasks, not diagnostic certainty.

03Breeding lifecycle decision supportUmayanga L.G.H.D. · IT21802294+

One lifecycle system per tank or pair considers breeder observations, IoT history, image evidence and timing. Its research covers readiness, eggs and fry, with rules and explanations kept separate from learned predictions.

FunctionAI / logicExplainability & output
Breeding readinessXGBoost on behaviour, IoT statistics and history; four reproductive phasesSHAP; phase, confidence and evidence where supported
Egg analyzerEfficientNet-B0 on healthy, unhealthy and fungal appearance; egg age and context rulesGrad-CAM; egg-condition interpretation and trend context
Fry careStage logic, Isolation Forest mortality-anomaly concepts and time-series forecastingStage, anomalies and forecast information where validated
Safety & adviceDeterministic checks and retrieval-grounded explanations after the relevant decisionRule trace, source support and an action plan

Research aim

Support repeatable decisions across pre-spawning, egg and fry stages using tank identity, timestamps, observations and relevant history. Tank Profile retains context; Quick Check discloses missing history.

Evaluation status

Readiness scenarios, egg-image tests, fry anomalies and forecasts require task-specific validation. The integrated report does not claim a single independently verified breeding-pipeline accuracy or exact spawning time.

04Fish measurement, biomass & crowdingCooray M.S.S. · IT22295606+

Calibrated imagery supports estimates of fish dimensions and biomass rather than relying on fish count alone. Separate models assess current crowding risk and projected loading over time.

FunctionData / methodResult
Fish measurementThree tank photographs, visible 5 × 5 cm blue marker and YOLOv8n bounding boxesCalibrated fish length and height estimates
Biomass estimationEllipsoid-inspired geometric approximation and conversion assumptionsEstimated volume, mass and tank loading
Current crowdingMonotonic XGBoost classifier on derived loading featuresPresent crowding risk category
Future loadingRandom Forest biomass regression at 14-day intervals up to 84 daysProjected biomass and future loading interpretation

Research aim

Improve fish-size-aware stocking decisions with calibrated visual evidence and physically interpretable biomass and crowding indicators.

Recorded component evidence

Fish detection mAP@50: 97.9%; current crowding accuracy: 92.35%; 14-day biomass forecast MAE: 1.974 g. Each comes from a separate evaluation task.

The four components use different datasets, labels and evaluation metrics. Recorded component scores are not a single overall NurtureAqua accuracy. Full technical procedures and additional limitations belong in the individual theses and integrated report.

Sensing

Supported ESP32 acquisition provides selected readings, including temperature and pH, alongside manual inputs when available. Readings must retain timestamps and data-source labels.

Backend

The four analytical branches connect through a shared React Native/Expo client and FastAPI service routes; deployed capabilities and service contracts remain component-specific.

Component-level evaluation evidence

Disease species-verification test accuracy: 98.17%; disease YOLOv8s validation mAP@50: 68.37%. Fish measurement YOLOv8n detection mAP@50: 97.9%; current crowding classification accuracy: 92.35%; 14-day biomass regression MAE: 1.974 g. Water numerical-risk accuracy in draft notes requires notebook verification, and no universal breeding or whole-system score is established. These are separate experiments and should not be averaged.
